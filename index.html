Konstantinos Emmanouilidis

PhD student in machine learning, optimization, and the theory of AI.

I am a PhD student in the Department of Computer and Information Science at the University of Pennsylvania, advised by René Vidal and affiliated with the GRASP Laboratory. My research interests lie in the theoretical foundations of deep learning and AI, machine learning, optimization, and theoretical computer science.

Before coming to the United States, I completed a Diploma in Electrical and Computer Engineering in Greece, where I worked with Dimitris Fotakis and Pavlos Efraimidis on problems at the intersection of opinion dynamics and theoretical computer science.

More broadly, I am interested in mathematically principled ways of understanding modern learning systems, especially through dynamics, robustness, and geometric structure.

Research Motto Towards a theoretical foundation of modern AI.

Optimization dynamics

Understanding the behavior of stochastic algorithms in modern overparameterized regimes, including stability, implicit bias, and large-step-size phenomena.

02

Robust and certifiable learning

Designing classifiers and latent-space methods with principled robustness guarantees, especially through geometric and probabilistic structure.

03

Theory for modern AI systems

Building mathematical frameworks for deep networks and related AI systems, with an emphasis on interpretable structure and provable performance.
